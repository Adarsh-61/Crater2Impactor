Estimating Impactor Properties from Crater Observations

When an impactor strikes the lunar surface, its energy excavates a crater. If we know the impactor's relevant properties (size, speed, and density), we can estimate the resulting crater dimensions using physical scaling relationships.

However, when studying the Moon, we face the opposite problem: we observe a crater and need to figure out what kind of impactor formed it. Because different combinations of an impactor's size and speed can produce the same crater, this "inverse problem" has no single answer. The system calculates the probability distribution of all impactors consistent with the crater and the stated assumptions. The crater diameter fixes a combination of mass and speed; it does not fix the speed, impact angle or impactor density, so those are reported as assumptions unless independent measurements are supplied.

Auxiliary measurements

A known impact angle, or the ejecta pattern of a fresh crater, constrains the angle. Ejecta radius and melt volume are compared with the model's expectations but do not constrain the impactor, because no calibrated measurement model exists for them. Blocky ejecta constrains the regolith thickness.
