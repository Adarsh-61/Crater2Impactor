Validation and Statistical Reliability

Tests of the inference on synthetic craters, laboratory craters and measured lunar impacts, and a comparison with the previous version of this application.

Summary

Synthetic tests check that the calculation is correct and its uncertainty calibrated when the model is right. Only the laboratory and lunar measurements test the model against nature, and no natural lunar crater has an independently known impactor, so none of these numbers is an accuracy for natural impacts.

Calibration on Synthetic Craters

Synthetic craters are drawn from the model's own stated assumptions, and the posterior is computed for each. If the uncertainty is calibrated, the true value falls inside the 68% and 90% ranges about 68% and 90% of the time.

Measured Lunar Impacts

The only lunar craters whose impactors are known independently are those made by spacecraft. All of them were hollow and slow (1.6 to 2.6 km/s), unlike natural impactors, so these cases test the scaling law outside the conditions it was built for.

From the diameter alone, the current model underestimates these masses more than the previous version did. The cause is the assumed natural impact speed, about seven times too fast for spacecraft: given the measured speed, the error falls from … to … dex. The previous version's smaller error came from two compensating assumptions: the same too-fast speeds, and a 30 MPa basalt target for these 20 to 35 m mare craters, which form in regolith. With the true speeds, the previous model overestimates the masses about eightfold. For known artificial impacts, a separate spacecraft population can be selected on the Inverse page.

Production Rate of New Lunar Craters

New craters found by comparing images of the same areas taken years apart give a crater production rate. Combined with the independently measured rate of small asteroid impacts on Earth, scaled to the Moon, the scaling law predicts how many new craters of each size should appear. No model parameter was adjusted to these counts.
